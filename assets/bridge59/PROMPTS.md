# Bridge/lake study 59 generated artwork

Mode: built-in `image_gen.imagegen`, not CLI/API fallback. The two bridge kits used `transparent_background=true`; water used false. All final PNGs were copied into this directory, leaving the original generated files in place.

Input images (style/material references, not edit targets):
- `res://assets/house16/slate.png`: cottage paint style, contour and colour reference.
- `res://assets/garden45/wood.png`: existing painted wood material reference.

Outputs: `painted_wood_kit.png`, `painted_stone_kit.png`, `painted_water.png`.

The original alpha is preserved. Runtime atlas regions select the three parts; no background removal or artwork repainting was performed by a shell/image script. `shore_distance.png` is a separate numeric polygon-distance texture made by `tools/bake_lakeshore59.py`, not generated illustration.

This is NOT a screenshot or concept scene. Exactly three isolated parts vertically stacked in three equal-height rows on transparent background, with generous clear gutters. Top row: a single repeating 3.6-metre-wide timber railing module, viewed square-on along the bridge side, no isometric yaw, camera looking down by 25 degrees; two stout end posts and TWO gently sagging rope rails between them, small moss patches, no foliage blocking the open middle. Middle row: a straight rectangular walkable wood plank DECK MATERIAL PATCH viewed perfectly top-down, edges parallel to image edges, about 3.6 metres wide left-to-right by 4 metres deep; transverse planks run top-to-bottom, narrow seams, lightly worn honey brown, no rails or objects. Bottom row: a repeating horizontal side-beam/fascia module, about 3.6m long and .6m high, painterly hand-hewn wood with dark lower lip and sparse moss. All three modules fill about 85% of the image width and are horizontally centred. Keep object sides straight, modular endpoints flush; consistent neutral diffuse daylight, no cast ground shadow or baked bright spotlight. Real transparent alpha, not a checkerboard image. No water, land, scene, people, text, labels, border, icons or perspective vanishing point. Atlas 1536x1024 or comparably detailed landscape format.
